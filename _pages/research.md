  <div class="research-heading">

    <h2 class="research-title">
      Quantum Theory
    </h2>

    <div class="research-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="research-content">

    <p class="research-description">
      I am drawn to the geometric and topological side of quantum theory, especially to those situations in which electromagnetic interactions seem to conspire with geometry and topology. Gauge structure, nonlocality, electromagnetic duality and magnetic charge all belong to this picture. To me, the geometrisation of interaction is one of the most elegant ideas in quantum theory.
    </p>


    <div class="research-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 24
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          The Aharonov–Bohm effect in a closed flux line
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An exact formulation of the Aharonov–Bohm effect for a closed
          magnetic flux line, providing a natural idealisation of a toroidal
          magnet. The AB phase is determined by a linking number rather than
          a winding number, making its topological character explicit and
          allowing the local and nonlocal interpretations of the effect to
          be examined from a different perspective.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>The European Physical Journal Plus</em>,
          <b>137</b>, 641 (2022)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-title">
          The quantum phase of a dyon
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A duality-invariant quantum phase acquired by a dyon encircling
          confined electric and magnetic fluxes. The construction unifies
          the Aharonov–Bohm phase with its electromagnetic dual and shows
          how topology, nonlocality and electric–magnetic duality can be
          incorporated within a single quantum-mechanical framework.
        </div>

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Dyons with phase
          δ<sub>θ</sub> = nθ
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An extension of the topological dyon phase obtained by combining
          electromagnetic duality with the Witten effect. The resulting
          phase is proportional to the vacuum angle θ and becomes quantised
          as δ<sub>θ</sub> = nθ, establishing a connection between dyonic
          quantum phases, CP violation and an Abelian form of the θ-vacua.
        </div>

           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Dirac Quantization Condition Holds with Nonzero Photon Mass
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An investigation of whether the Dirac quantisation condition
          survives when the photon is allowed to have a nonzero mass.
          Although the usual electromagnetic-angular-momentum argument is
          then modified by screening, the gauge-theoretic argument survives:
          magnetic charge cannot be screened, and the invisibility of the
          Dirac string or the equivalent Wu–Yang gauge transition continues
          to require charge quantisation.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Alfred S. Goldhaber and Ricardo Heras,
          <em>arXiv</em>:1710.03321 (2017)

  <div class="research-heading">

    <h2 class="research-title">
      Classical Electrodynamics
    </h2>

    <div class="research-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="research-content">

    <p class="research-description">
      I am interested in what classical electrodynamics can reveal when
      electromagnetic systems are considered in non-simply connected
      spaces. In particular, I have explored how topology, nonlocality,
      electromagnetic angular momentum and electric–magnetic duality
      become intertwined even within a classical field theory.
    </p>


    <div class="research-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 22
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Can classical electrodynamics predict nonlocal effects?
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A study of electromagnetic angular momentum in the configuration
          formed by a charge encircling a confined magnetic flux. Although
          classical electrodynamics is fundamentally local, the resulting
          angular momentum is topological, depends on a winding number and
          connects spatially separated electromagnetic regions. Its magnitude
          provides a classical counterpart of the Aharonov–Bohm phase.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          José A. Heras and Ricardo Heras,
          <em>The European Physical Journal Plus</em>,
          <b>136</b>, 847 (2021)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh22.pdf">

            DOI

        </div>

      </div>


      <!-- ==========================================

           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Topology, nonlocality and duality in classical electrodynamics
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An extension of topological electromagnetic angular momentum to
          systems containing both electric and magnetic charges and fluxes.
          A dyon interacting with a dual solenoid gives rise to an angular
          momentum that is simultaneously topological, nonlocal and invariant
          under electromagnetic duality transformations, unifying electric
          and magnetic versions of the construction.
        </div>

  <div class="research-heading">

    <h2 class="research-title">
      High-Energy Astrophysics
    </h2>

    <div class="research-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="research-content">

    <p class="research-description">
      I no longer work on neutron-star astrophysics, but it was the subject
      through which I first found my way into physics. My early work explored
      the violent birth of neutron stars, particularly the possible connections
      between magnetic-field evolution, rapid rotation, magnetorotational
      instabilities and the unusually large space velocities observed in
      pulsars.
    </p>


    <div class="research-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 8
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Magnetorotational instabilities and pulsar kick velocities
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A model connecting the large observed space velocities of neutron
          stars with magnetorotational instabilities occurring shortly after
          their birth. The proposed mechanism converts energy stored in
          differential rotation into radiation and translational kinetic
          energy on an Alfvén timescale, linking pulsar kicks with millisecond
          rotation and very strong magnetic fields.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>New Astronomy</em>,
          <b>43</b>, 6–9 (2016)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh8.pdf">

          </a>

            DOI
          </a>

        </div>

      </div>


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 4
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="research-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Birth accelerations of neutron stars
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An early model of neutron-star birth in which rotational-energy
          loss, magnetic-field decay and the acquisition of space velocity
          are treated as interconnected processes. The resulting energy
          balance suggests that newly born neutron stars may undergo
          extremely large accelerations during a short and highly energetic
          stage of their evolution.
        </div>
