<section class="teaching-section">

  <div class="teaching-heading">

    <h2 class="teaching-title">
      Classical Electrodynamics
    </h2>

    <div class="teaching-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="teaching-content">

    <p class="teaching-description">
      These papers explore alternative mathematical and pedagogical routes
      through classical electrodynamics, with particular emphasis on
      Helmholtz theorems, causality, retarded fields and electromagnetic
      potentials.
    </p>


    <div class="teaching-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 10
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          The Helmholtz theorem and retarded fields
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An examination of how the Helmholtz theorem can be extended from
          static vector fields to time-dependent electromagnetic fields.
          The paper distinguishes instantaneous and causal formulations and
          shows how suitable generalisations of the theorem lead naturally
          to the retarded fields of Maxwell's equations.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>European Journal of Physics</em>,
          <b>37</b>, 065204 (2016)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh10.pdf">
            PDF
          </a>

          &nbsp;·&nbsp;

          <a href="https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/6/065204">
            DOI
          </a>

        </div>

      </div>


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 13
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Alternative routes to the retarded potentials
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A comparison of two routes to the retarded electromagnetic
          potentials: the standard Lorenz-gauge construction and an
          alternative derivation based on the retarded Helmholtz theorem.
          The discussion also contrasts retarded and instantaneous
          potentials and extends the construction to electrodynamics
          with magnetic monopoles.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>European Journal of Physics</em>,
          <b>38</b>, 055203 (2017)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh13.pdf">
            PDF
          </a>

          &nbsp;·&nbsp;

          <a href="https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aa7f18">
            DOI
          </a>

        </div>

      </div>


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 19
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Helmholtz's theorem for two retarded fields and its application
          to Maxwell's equations
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A two-field extension of the Helmholtz theorem in which a pair of
          retarded vector fields is determined by their divergences and
          coupled curls. Applied to Maxwell's equations, the theorem provides
          a direct route from the field equations to the retarded electric
          and magnetic fields.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          José A. Heras and Ricardo Heras,
          <em>European Journal of Physics</em>,
          <b>41</b>, 035201 (2020)

<section class="teaching-section">

  <div class="teaching-heading">

    <h2 class="teaching-title">
      Special Relativity
    </h2>

    <div class="teaching-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="teaching-content">

    <p class="teaching-description">
      These papers present elementary routes to the Lorentz transformations,
      emphasising the geometrical structure of spacetime and the special
      role played by the wave equation and the d'Alembert operator.
    </p>


    <div class="teaching-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 9
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Lorentz transformations and the wave equation
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A compact derivation of the Lorentz transformations obtained by
          demanding form invariance of the d'Alembert operator between
          inertial frames. The construction provides an alternative
          introduction to special relativity in which spacetime symmetry
          emerges directly from the structure of the wave equation.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>European Journal of Physics</em>,
          <b>37</b>, 025603 (2016)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh9.pdf">
            PDF
          </a>

          &nbsp;·&nbsp;

          <a href="https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/37/2/025603">
            DOI
          </a>

        </div>

      </div>


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 11
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Four easy routes to the Lorentz transformations:
          addendum to 'Lorentz transformations and the wave equation'
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          Four accessible derivations of the Lorentz transformations are
          compared. Two begin with invariance of the Minkowski interval and
          two with invariance of the d'Alembert operator, illustrating how
          apparently different starting points encode the same spacetime
          symmetry.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,

          <b>38</b>, 019401 (2017)

      Quantum Mechanics
    </h2>

    <div class="teaching-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="teaching-content">

    <p class="teaching-description">

      electromagnetism, gauge invariance and magnetic monopoles.
    </p>


    <div class="teaching-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================

        <div class="paper-title">
          Dirac quantisation condition: a comprehensive review
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A pedagogical review of the Dirac quantisation condition and its
          underlying physics. Gauge invariance, singular potentials,
          single-valued wave functions, the Dirac string and electromagnetic
          angular momentum are developed through several quantum-mechanical
          and semiclassical derivations of charge quantisation.
        </div>

  <div class="teaching-heading">

    <h2 class="teaching-title">
      History of Physics
    </h2>

    <div class="teaching-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="teaching-content">

    <p class="teaching-description">
      These papers revisit episodes in the conceptual development of
      electromagnetism and special relativity, particularly Feynman's
      approach to electromagnetic potentials and the early role of the
      wave equation in the emergence of spacetime transformations.
    </p>


    <div class="teaching-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 20
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          On Feynman's handwritten notes on electromagnetism and the idea
          of introducing potentials before fields
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An exploration of Feynman's 1963 handwritten notes on an
          alternative formulation of electrodynamics. Motivated by his idea
          of introducing electromagnetic potentials before the fields, the
          paper develops a route from charge conservation and causality to
          the retarded potentials and ultimately to Maxwell's equations.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          José A. Heras and Ricardo Heras,
          <em>European Journal of Physics</em>,
          <b>41</b>, 035202 (2020)

           Article 21

          Reply to comments 'On Feynman's handwritten notes on
          electromagnetism and the idea of introducing potentials before fields'
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A response to comments on the interpretation and implementation
          of Feynman's handwritten electrodynamics notes. The discussion
          clarifies the conceptual motivation for introducing potentials
          before fields and addresses objections to the proposed

<section class="teaching-section">

  <div class="teaching-heading">

    <h2 class="teaching-title">
      Essays and Philosophical Reflections
    </h2>

    <div class="teaching-divider"></div>

  </div>


  <div class="teaching-content">

    <p class="teaching-description">
      These essays reflect on independent thought, creativity and the
      teaching of physics, and on the intellectual conditions that allow
      students to engage with physics as an exploration of nature rather
      than simply as a collection of techniques to be mastered.
    </p>


    <div class="teaching-papers">


      <!-- ==========================================
           Article 5
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Individualism: The legacy of great physicists
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          An essay on the role of independent thought in physics and its
          relationship with curiosity, doubt, imagination and creativity.
          Drawing on the examples of major physicists, it argues that
          fruitful collaboration is strengthened rather than weakened by
          the intellectual independence of the individuals involved.
        </div>

        <div class="paper-citation">
          Ricardo Heras,
          <em>Physics Today</em>, online (2013)
        </div>

        <div class="paper-links">

          <a href="https://ricardoheras.github.io/assets/pdf/rh5.pdf">

            DOI

        </div>

           Article 12
           ========================================== -->

      <div class="teaching-paper">

        <div class="paper-title">
          Commentary: How to teach me physics:
          Tradition is not always a virtue
        </div>

        <div class="paper-summary">
          A student perspective on physics education that questions an
          excessive reliance on rote learning, standard exercises and
          examinations. The essay argues instead for a form of education
          centred on free inquiry, independent reasoning and what Feynman
